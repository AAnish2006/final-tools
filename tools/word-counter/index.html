About Our Online Word Counter

The DevToolBox Word Counter gives live word, character, sentence, paragraph, keyword-frequency, and reading-time estimates. Ordinary character totals count Unicode grapheme clusters, so combining marks and joined emoji are not split into multiple visible characters. Selected X posts use weighted units: recognized HTTP(S) URLs count as 23 units, and many non-Latin characters and emoji count as 2. Other selected platform limits use grapheme counts. The checker is an estimate: X recognizes explicit HTTP(S) URLs, while bare domains and some URL punctuation may be counted differently from X. Platform rules and Unicode handling can change, so verify important drafts in the destination app.

Documented platform limits

  • X: Standard posts (280 weighted units), longer posts (25,000 weighted units), profile bio (160 characters), and display name (50 characters).
  • LinkedIn: Posts (3,000 characters) and profile headlines (220 characters).
  • YouTube: Video titles (100 characters) and descriptions (5,000 characters).
  • Pinterest: Pin titles (100 characters) and descriptions (800 characters).

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the X post length limits?

X standard posts have a 280-unit weighted limit, while longer posts can have a 25,000-unit limit for eligible subscribers. This checker estimates X weighted units: recognized HTTP(S) URLs count as 23 units, and many non-Latin characters and emoji count as 2. Bare domains and edge-case URL punctuation may differ from X, so verify important drafts in the destination app.

How does the red overflow highlighting work?

When the text exceeds the selected limit, an overflow preview marks the excess text. The trim action keeps content up to the selected limit without splitting grapheme clusters; on X, it also keeps recognized URLs intact.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time is estimated based on the standard average adult reading speed of 200 words per minute (wpm).

What is keyword density in this word counter?

Keyword frequency shows how often a term appears in this draft. It is a descriptive editing aid, not a Google ranking score or a recommended target percentage.
